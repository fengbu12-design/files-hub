Endogenous production and firm problem

Firm Production
Representative firm produces according to:
Yt = AtF(Kt, Nt)
Kt: capital input
Nt: labor input
At: exogenous productivity
F(·) satisfies the usual set of assumptions:

F(γK, γN) = γF(K, N)

===== SLIDE PAGE 12 =====
Household
Representative household gets utility from consumption and
leisure, where leisure is 1 −N.
Lifetime utility:
U = u(Ct, 1 −Nt) + βu(Ct+1, 1 −Nt+1)
Assume positive and diminishing marginal utilities:
uC > 0, uL > 0, uCC < 0, uLL < 0.

===== SLIDE PAGE 13 =====
GHH Preferences
We assume flow utility function u(·) takes the following form:
u(Ct, 1 −Nt) = ln [Ct + θt ln(1 −Nt)]
Parameter θt represents how much household values leisure relative
to consumption.
Known as GHH preferences, credited to Greenwood, Hercowitz,
and Huffman (1988).
This functional form will greatly simplify our analysis by
eliminating wealth effects from the labor supply decision.

===== SLIDE PAGE 17 =====
Household Optimality Conditions: Interpretation
Ct condition is the usual consumption Euler equation, only looks
different to accommodate utility from leisure.
Nt condition says: marginal benefit of taking one more unit of
leisure (RHS) is equal to marginal cost, captured by foregone wage
income and evaluated in units of marginal utility of consumption
(LHS).
Nt+1 condition looks exactly the same as Nt, only dated t + 1.

===== SLIDE PAGE 24 =====
Summary
Endogenous: Ct, Nt, It, Yt, wt, rt.
Exogenous: At, At+1, Kt, θt.
Three markets: labor, loanable funds, goods.
Two prices: wt, rt (relative to final good).
Endowment economy special case with Nt and It fixed.
Will be possible to do some consumption smoothing in equilibrium
here. Suppose household wants to increase St. Can do so if rt falls
to incentivize more It (whereas in endowment economy It = 0 so
St must remain fixed at 0).
