===== SLIDE PAGE 2 =====
Introduction
The equilibrium model with production and labor supply that we
have been working with forms the “real block” of the neoclassical
or real business cycle (RBC) model.
Model features optimizing agents and frictionless markets.
It emphasizes supply shocks (changes in At or θt) as the principal
drivers of fluctuations in endogenous variables.
The “nominal block” does not drive fluctuations as the
classical dichotomy holds in this model, which we will discuss next
time.
We take the model to be a relevant description of the real world in
the medium run – between a couple of years and a decade.

===== SLIDE PAGE 4 =====
Equilibrium Conditions
First four are optimal decision rules of household and firm.
Fifth is a technological constraint captured by production function.
Sixth is a resource constraint or goods market clearing condition.
Exogenous variables: {At, At+1, Gt, Gt+1, Kt, θt}.
Endogenous variables: {Ct, Nt, It, Yt, wt, rt}.
Take Yt+1 as “pseudo-exogenous” – not affected by It, which
impacts Kt+1.
Medium-run assumption: treat capital stock as roughly constant.

===== SLIDE PAGE 5 =====
Graphing the Equilibrium
IS curve: set of (rt, Yt) pairs where household and firm behave
optimally and aggregate desired expenditure equals income.
Summarizes consumption function, investment demand
function, and the resource constraint. [Demand Side]
Y s curve: set of (rt, Yt) pairs where household and firm behave
optimally and labor market clears and production function holds.
Summarizes labor supply, labor demand, and production
function. [Supply Side]
General equilibrium: on both IS curve and Y s curve
simultaneously.

Shifts in IS Curve
IS curve will shift if any exogenous variable relevant for desired
consumption or investment changes, or if there are changes in
government spending:
↑At+1: IS shifts right
↑Gt: IS shifts right
↑Gt+1: IS shifts left
↓Kt: IS shifts right

Begin by plotting labor demand and labor supply. Find the Nt

===== SLIDE PAGE 16 =====
Bringing It All Together
In general equilibrium, economy must be on both IS and Y s curves.
Labor market clearing embedded in Y s curve
Goods / financial market clearing embedded in IS curve
Intersection jointly determines Yt, rt, Nt, wt.
To determine Ct and It, use the consumption and investment
demand functions.

===== SLIDE PAGE 18 =====
Effects of Changes in Exogenous Variables
At, θt, Kt affect the position of the Y s curve.
At+1, Gt, Gt+1, Kt affect the position of the IS curve.
Figure out how Y s and IS curves shift, determine rt. Use this to
figure out how other endogenous variables react.
One complication: changes in It affect Kt+1, which affects Yt+1
and hence Ct.
We ignore these effects – size of capital stock is large relative to
investment, and in medium run can treat capital stock as
approximately fixed.
Yt+1 will therefore only be affected by changes in exogenous
variables dated t + 1, i.e., At+1 and Gt+1. “Pseudo-exogenous” in
the sense we treat it as unaffected by time t exogenous shocks.

===== SLIDE PAGE 23 =====
Supply vs. Demand
With a vertical Y s curve, output is completely supply-determined.
“Demand shocks” (shocks that shift the IS curve) affect
composition of output and rt, but not the level of output.
Neoclassical model thus emphasizes supply shocks (productivity
and labor preferences) as chief source of fluctuations.
Can get demand shocks to impact output if Y s curve is
upward-sloping (i.e., interest rate affects labor supply), but doesn’t
change fact that model still needs to be predominantly driven by
supply shocks to make predictions more or less consistent with
data.
