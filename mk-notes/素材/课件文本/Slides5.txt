===== SLIDE PAGE 3 =====
Modes of Economic Analysis
1. Decision Theory: derivation of optimal decision rules, taking
prices as given
e.g., consumption function, labor demand function, etc.
2. Partial Equilibrium: determine price that clears one market,
taking prices in all other markets as given
e.g., wages and the labor market, interest rates and the
loanable funds market
3. General Equilibrium: simultaneously determine all prices to
simultaneously clear all markets
e.g., simultaneously determine wage and interest rate to clear
the labor and loanable funds markets
Macro is really about general equilibrium.

===== SLIDE PAGE 4 =====
Competitive Equilibrium
A competitive equilibrium of an economy is a set of prices and
allocations where (i) all agents behave according to decision rules
taking prices as given, and (ii) all markets simultaneously clear.
In this lecture, we consider equilibrium in an endowment economy
with no endogenous production, i.e., with exogenous income.
With fixed quantities, it becomes particularly clear how price
adjustment results in equilibrium.

Competitive Equilibrium: Endowment Economy
Equilibrium in an endowment economy entails:
Optimal decision rule: consumption function
Market: loanable funds/savings, St
Price: real interest rate rt
Market clearing: in aggregate, saving is zero, or equivalently,
Yt = Ct
Allocations: Ct, Ct+1

IS Curve
IS curve: the set of (rt, Yt) pairs where income equals expenditure
and is consistent with optimal behavior by households.
IS stands for “Investment equals Saving.”
Summarizes demand side of the economy.
Negative relationship between rt and Yt.

===== SLIDE PAGE 17 =====
Interpreting Supply Shock Effect
When Yt goes up, equilibrium rt falls.
Lower interest rate shifts the expenditure line up in a way that
income equals expenditure at the new level of Yt.
Intuitively, when current income goes up, household would like to
increase consumption in both periods (due to consumption
smoothing).
For that, household would like to save more. But, in equilibrium
this is impossible since no one else wants to borrow.
As a result, interest rate must fall to dissuade households from
increasing savings.

===== SLIDE PAGE 19 =====
Interpreting Demand Shock Effect
When Yt+1 goes up, expenditure line shifts up on impact, and IS
curve shifts out.
Since Y s curve is unchanged, equilibrium interest rate rises.
Expenditure line shifts back down to where it began.
Intuitively, when future income is expected to increase, households
would like to increase current consumption by borrowing
(consumption smoothing).
But in equilibrium, households cannot increase borrowing.
Hence, rt must rise to dissuade additional borrowing.

===== SLIDE PAGE 20 =====
Discussion
Market clearing requires Ct = Yt.
For a given rt, household does not want Ct = Yt. Wants to
smooth consumption relative to income.
But in equilibrium cannot smooth without aggregate saving.
rt adjusts so that household is content to have Ct = Yt.
rt ends up being a measure of how plentiful the future is expected
to be relative to the present.
